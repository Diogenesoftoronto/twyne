## Research report: interaction motion for Twyne

### 1. Game feel and juice applied to productivity UI
- **Juice it or lose it (Jonasson and Purho, GDC 2012).** They take a plain Breakout and keep the rules fixed. They then add layers in order: colour, tweening and easing, squash and stretch, sound, particles, screen shake, then faces. Takeaway: feedback comes in layers on top of rules that don't change, so juice is presentation and never changes outcomes. https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/juice-it-or-lose
- **Screen shake causes motion sickness.** Later write-ups say "a shake should be a punch, not an earthquake." Shake a stamp, never the viewport. https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/juice-it-or-lose (Nijman's "Art of Screenshake" talk covers the same ground)
- **Emil Kowalski: animation budget scales with how often an action happens.** Rules from his STANDARDS.md:
  - Used 100+ times a day: no animation.
  - Used tens of times a day: remove or minimise.
  - Occasional (modals, toasts): a standard animation.
  - Rare (onboarding, celebrations): delight is allowed.
  - Keyboard-triggered actions are never animated. Raycast is his example.
  https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills/blob/main/skills/review-animations/STANDARDS.md
- **Kowalski's numbers.**
  - UI animations stay under 300ms. 200ms is the default.
  - Press feedback is 100–160ms, for example `transform 160ms ease-out` with scale 0.97.
  - Tooltips take 125ms, and every tooltip after the first shows instantly.
  - Drawers take about 500ms with `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`.
  - Stagger items by 30–80ms.
  - Use ease-out for entering elements and avoid ease-in, which feels slow.
  - Use blur (around 2px) only as a last resort to smooth a crossfade.
  https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips
- **Rauno Freiberg, "Invisible details of interaction design".**
  - Motion should be interruptible: you can reverse it mid-gesture and never have to wait for it to finish.
  - Thrown objects keep their momentum, and bounce matches how heavy the object should feel.
  - A threshold commits an action. Before the threshold, the response tracks the input.
  - "Fidgetability" means small satisfying responses invite people to repeat them.
  - Scroll stays with the pane where it started.
  Takeaway: margin notes and stamps should be draggable and reversible before they commit. https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design
- **Linear's timing.** This comes from a third-party reading of Linear's stylesheet, not from Linear itself. Highlights fade in over 0s and out over 0.15s. The quick, regular and slow transitions are 0.1s, 0.25s and 0.35s. Popovers appear instantly and fade out on dismiss. Width, height and margin are never animated. Takeaway: appear instantly, leave softly. https://performance.dev/how-is-linear-so-fast-a-technical-breakdown
- **Raycast's bar is "feeling native".** Their deep dive lists: no flicker when views appear, no `cursor: pointer` on controls, and the WebView finishes drawing before the window is shown. https://www.raycast.com/blog/a-technical-deep-dive-into-the-new-raycast
- **Apple HIG.** Avoid motion on UI interactions people repeat often. Avoid oscillation near 0.2 Hz (one cycle every five seconds); if something must oscillate, keep the amplitude low and the content translucent. This matters for any "breathing" idle state. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion
- **Vercel interface guidelines.**
  - Animate only `transform` and `opacity`, and never use `transition: all`.
  - Every animation must be interruptible.
  - Motion that autoplays for more than 5 seconds needs a pause control.
  - Decorative loops stop under reduced motion.
  - Per-keystroke work must stay cheap.
  https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/command.md

### 2. Editors with a distinctive typing, caret or scroll feel
- **Word's gliding caret (since Office 2013) is widely disliked.**
  - On Hacker News: "that cursor animation is just awful… laggy animation on top of what should be instant."
  - Word's own "Provide feedback with animation" checkbox doesn't turn it off. The only fixes are the Windows-wide animation setting (which some Windows updates turn back on) or the `DisableAnimations` registry value.
  Takeaway: never delay the caret on a keypress. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19686799
- **VS Code keeps the caret instant while typing.** `editor.cursorSmoothCaretAnimation` has the values off, explicit and on. "explicit" animates only when you jump with a click or navigation, not while typing. Adopt that split. https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/169332
- **Monkeytype** has `smoothCaret: off | slow | medium | fast`, and the speed setting came from users saying the glide was too slow. Typing-test users accept a smooth caret only when it is fast and optional. Source for the options: https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype (packages/schemas/src/configs.ts). Source for the complaint: https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/discussions/1248
- **iA Writer's Focus Mode** dims everything except the current sentence or paragraph, and Typewriter Mode keeps the current line vertically centred. Both are toggles, and the dimming is a colour change, not movement. https://ia.net/writer/support/editor/focus-mode
- **Ulysses, Typora, Obsidian, Bear.**
  - Ulysses' typewriter mode, with a configurable line position and a line highlight, is a fan favourite.
  - Typora has Focus (F8) and Typewriter (F9) modes.
  - Obsidian gets the same through the obsidian-typewriter-mode plugin, which offers line position as a percentage, a highlight style and paragraph dimming.
  - Bear users still have an open feature request for typewriter mode.
  Takeaway: writers value stillness and a predictable eye line more than motion. Unverified: Ulysses' exact options, because its help page now redirects.
- **Paper by FiftyThree (now WeTransfer)** drew stroke width from drawing speed rather than pressure, on a custom OpenGL engine; a later update advertised three-times-faster ink. A strong precedent for ink whose physics comes from speed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FiftyThree
- **Teenage Engineering's OP-1** gives each mode its own colour and animated graphic, so the playful visuals tell you which mode you are in. https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/teenage-engineering
- **Not verified:** Craft's and Hemingway's motion. Hemingway uses static colour highlights only.

### 3. Ink, paper and letterpress on the web
- **SVG filter recipes for type (Carmen Ansio).**
  - Ink gain: `feMorphology dilate`, then `feFlood`, then `feComposite in`.
  - Rough, bled edges: `feTurbulence` into `feDisplacementMap`. `fractalNoise` gives softer edges than `turbulence`.
  - Pitfalls: turbulence, displacement and lighting are the expensive primitives. Defs inside a `display:none` SVG are dropped in Chromium. Set `color-interpolation-filters="sRGB"`. CSS cannot animate filter primitive attributes, so drive changes from JS with requestAnimationFrame and an IntersectionObserver.
  https://www.carmenansio.com/articles/svg-filters-on-type/
- **SVG filter performance.**
  - Keep the filter region tight. `feTurbulence` is unbounded and defaults to a region of 120%.
  - Never animate transform on a filtered element, because the filter is recomputed every frame.
  - Pre-render the result to a bitmap.
  - CSS filter functions are much cheaper than SVG filters.
  Rule for Twyne: never put a live filter on the editor surface. Use filters only on small, short-lived elements such as a stamp. https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/02/19/svg-filter-effects-creating-texture-with-feturbulence/
- **Paper grain.** Generate the texture once (feTurbulence or a shader) and cache it as a tiled background. Lighting by a moving light is best done as a fragment shader on a small canvas behind the text, at half or quarter resolution, re-rendered only when the light moves. I found no authoritative procedural paper shader; three.js normal-map approaches exist. https://discourse.threejs.org/t/distorted-grainy-material-due-to-a-normal-map/72599
- **Risograph and halftone.** Codrops has a risograph grain tutorial. glsl-halftone provides rotated dot grids. Mis-registration is a UV offset per spot-ink layer, which suits two inks, vermilion and cobalt.
  - https://tympanus.net/codrops/2022/03/07/creating-a-risograph-grain-light-effect-in-three-js/
  - https://github.com/glslify/glsl-halftone
- **Letterpress deboss in CSS** is a pair of `text-shadow` layers: light offset down, dark offset up. Animating the pair fakes a moving light. Note that `text-shadow` does not render on `background-clip:text`.
- **`backdrop-filter`** is costly inside scrolling content. I found no benchmark. Avoid it over the manuscript.

### 5. Motion that communicates consequence and trust
- **Undo beats "are you sure?"** People click through repeated warnings out of habit (Raskin). Gmail's Undo Send holds the message 5–30s. Use a visible delayed commit for "send to AI" or "publish". Keep heavy friction, like typing the item's name as GitHub does, for permanent destructive actions only. https://alistapart.com/article/neveruseawarning/
- **Hold-to-confirm.** Timings in component libraries range from 300ms to 2s. It is hard for people with tremor, so provide a single-activation path for keyboard and screen readers, and let releasing early cancel cleanly. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html
- **Disabled-with-reason.** Use `aria-disabled` rather than `disabled` so the control stays focusable, and explain why it is disabled. This fits out-of-credits and rate-limited states.
  - https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2021/08/frustrating-design-patterns-disabled-buttons/
  - https://uxdesign.cc/never-ever-disable-buttons-requirements-for-an-accessible-solution-acaf9c5191f9
- **Response-time limits (NN/g).**
  - Under 0.1s feels instant and needs no feedback.
  - About 1s keeps the user's train of thought; a subtle indicator helps.
  - Over about 10s needs real progress, such as percent done.
  Map AI waits onto these bands. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/
- **AI thinking states.**
  - Accepted patterns: show the current step and total steps, an elapsed counter ("Thinking for N s"), steps that collapse, and a clear "done" state.
  - Anti-pattern: fake progress shown before the model has started.
  - Text shimmer is now a generic AI cliché.
  - A stronger fit for Twyne is to show what an editor is reading, for example a marker moving through the paragraphs it has consumed.
  - Source: https://panelui.dev/docs/ai-components/shimmer, a component library. I found no authoritative critique of shimmer.

### 6. Ambient life and accessibility
- **Smooth remote cursors.** Fit a spline through position updates (about every 50–80ms) instead of using CSS transitions, which never finish when updates arrive irregularly. This is perfect-cursors, used in tldraw. Use it for any AI-editor presence marker. https://github.com/steveruizok/perfect-cursors
- **Figma shows your own edits before the server confirms them** and ignores conflicting incoming changes until then, to avoid flicker. The same applies to local versus synced state. https://www.figma.com/blog/how-figmas-multiplayer-technology-works/
- **WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA).** Motion triggered by interaction must be possible to disable unless it is essential. Colour, blur and opacity changes don't count as motion, so a reduced-motion mode can keep fades and ink-colour shifts. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html
- **Vestibular triggers.** Parallax, scroll-jacking, zoom, spinning, and large or full-screen motion. Never use parallax or scroll-linked movement in the manuscript; scroll-driven effects belong in the margin or chrome only. https://alistapart.com/article/designing-safer-web-animation-for-motion-sensitivity/
- **Opt in to motion.** Josh Comeau and Ben Myers suggest motion only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, plus an in-app override. https://benmyers.dev/encyclopedia/reduced-motion/
- **Time-of-day lighting.** Precedents are f.lux, Night Shift and dark mode by local sunlight (Hacker News, 2021). Change it slowly, never in steps, and keep text contrast fixed.

### 10 gaps that fit a literary editor with AI editors
1. **A caret that never glides on a keystroke** but leaves a brief wet-ink fade on each new glyph, done with highlight colour (no layout) and drying in about 300ms.
2. **Ink colour that tells you where text lives:** fresh ink for local only, dried ink once synced, a cobalt edge on text sent to a provider, and vermilion for published.
3. **Reading-progress presence for AI editors:** a quiet marker moving through the paragraphs each editor has read, with spline smoothing, instead of a shimmer or spinner.
4. **The rubric stamp:** a short ink-impression stamp with a halftone or mis-registration shader on a small canvas. It can be dragged and reversed until a threshold, and is fully undoable.
5. **"Send to the room" as a visible delayed commit:** the envelope sits on the desk for N seconds, and you can pull it back.
6. **Out-of-credits as an empty inkwell:** the control stays focusable and says why and when the credits refill.
7. **Margin notes that slide in from the margin edge,** grouped, with a stagger of 50ms or less. They never shift the manuscript layout.
8. **Paper grain lit by the time of day:** one cached texture plus a low-resolution light pass that only re-renders when the light changes. It pauses while you type.
9. **Destructive actions as a press imprint that deepens while held,** with a keyboard path and an undo window, rather than a modal.
10. **A Desk page where stats are set like a printer's composing stick:** movement happens only when you open the page, never during writing, and scroll effects stay out of the reading column.</result>
