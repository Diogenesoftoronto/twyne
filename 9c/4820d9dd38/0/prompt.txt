## Where Astra's argument is weakest, and how to answer it

Astra's own strongest objection: ordinary editing plus Undo may already cover trial editing. I think it does, for the trial itself. A textarea copy of the sentence is not a feature. What the editor cannot do is show the writer what their edit *did to the claim*. So the bench earns its place only if the semantic observation is real, and Astra's step 4 is the entire product. Two risks follow.

1. **Obvious observations.** "You removed 'may'" is a diff, not an observation. The model must be constrained to the *consequence* for the reader: what became definite, who now acts, what quantity or time shifted, what qualification elsewhere in the paragraph the edit now contradicts. If the model cannot name a consequence, it must return nothing, and the UI must show "No change in what this claims" rather than padding.
2. **Wrong observations.** A frontier prose model, not Jev or a fill-mask pack, does this read. Evidence spans are validated against code-owned text, and a validated span proves only that the quote exists. The writer can dismiss an observation in one click; dismissal is recorded for the eval corpus, never argued with.

## The one workflow: "Keep the original while you try"

**Entry**
- The selection toolbar shows **Try a revision** for a selection within one paragraph.
- The original is the exact selected range. Today's silent narrowing to one sentence is removed.
- Single-word selections no longer show **Alternatives**.
- The automatic struggle trigger can still open the bench. When it does, the earlier settled wordings from the ledger appear as chips that load into the trial. This is the only use of history, and an empty ledger just means no chips.

**Surface**
- The paragraph is always shown, with the selection in place. An **Original / Trial** toggle swaps the selection in the paragraph using the existing ghost preview.
- One editable trial, pre-filled with the original.
- A **Gone from the original** line, from the code layer. It updates as the writer types and needs no click and no network.
- Optionally, a model reading. It is requested with **Read the change**, or runs automatically once per distinct pair when the code layer flags a loss. It returns at most two observations. Each quotes the exact original and trial phrases, and an observation is dropped if its quotes don't match the text exactly. It never gives a score, a "safe" badge, or a rewrite. It only uses the writer's configured prose provider, never Jev or an on-device model.

**Decision**
- **Use revision**: one undoable transaction. Grammar findings are advice only and don't block it.
- **Keep original** (Esc).
- If the source changed while the bench was open, applying is blocked and the trial is kept for recovery.

**Cold start, realistic sentence**

> Most of the residents we interviewed said they would probably move if rents rose again, though few had looked at other neighbourhoods.

The writer selects it, chooses **Try a revision**, and tightens the trial to:

> Most residents said they would move if rents rose again.

Without any network, the bench shows:

> **Gone from the original:** "we interviewed" · "probably" · "though few had looked at other neighbourhoods"

If a provider is configured, the model reading adds:

> "would probably move" → "would move": the residents' intention now reads as firm. The dropped clause was the paragraph's evidence that the threat may be idle.

The writer can respond in three ways:
- Restore "probably" and use the revision.
- Keep the cut on purpose.
- Keep the original.

In each case they can still see the original while deciding.

**What makes it worth opening:** you can cut hard without losing the original, and you get a list of the qualifiers you dropped. Neither depends on the bench having seen any earlier versions.
