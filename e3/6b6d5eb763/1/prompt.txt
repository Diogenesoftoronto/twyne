Cloudflare released the preview October 19. It can return source-text spans with offsets, typed values and estimates. That makes it more interesting for your apps as an evidence-extraction component than as another general-purpose assistant. Release
My strongest fits:
1. Twyne: turn editorial feedback into connected evidence.
   For your multiple-quotation problem, keep exact/normalized string matching first. No model needed when the quotation matches. Clairic becomes useful for extracting all the relevant excerpts from a messy editor response or finding candidate passages behind a paraphrase. Then deterministic checks verify the text and attach it to the correct manuscript version. Repeated quotations still need disambiguation; an offset in the model’s input is not automatically a stable editor anchor.
That could make each persona card point to several passages, with Jev judging the relationship and the writing model explaining why the connection matters. It directly addresses your complaint that connecting passage A and B doesn’t explain what a writer should do with that connection.
2. Twyne: make auto-research produce something visible.
   Once search retrieves sources, extract their claims, supporting excerpts, dates and numerical findings into reviewable evidence cards. Jev decides whether a claim needs checking and whether a source supports or contradicts it; the writer model explains the significance.
The useful result isn’t “watching graph.” It’s “This sentence makes a factual claim; here are two relevant sources and the exact supporting passages.” Clairic would process retrieved material, not replace search or establish that a source is truthful.
